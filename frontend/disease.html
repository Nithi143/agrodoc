Fungal Pathogen High Severity

Early Blight

Alternaria solani

Verify with AI Scan
Pathogen Family Pleosporaceae
Primary Host Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)
Favorable Temp 24°C – 29°C (Warm & Humid)
Transmission Wind, Rain Splash, Tools

Pathology & Disease Overview

Early blight is one of the most destructive and widespread diseases of tomatoes worldwide. Caused by the necrotrophic fungus Alternaria solani, the disease targets foliar tissue, stems, and fruit. Under warm, wet conditions, infections can cause total premature defoliation, leaving tomatoes susceptible to sunscald and substantially diminishing marketable harvest yields.

Overwintering occurs in infected crop residue, solanaceous weeds (such as black nightshade), and volunteer tomato plants. Fungal spores (conidia) are readily transported by wind currents, rain splash, and mechanical farm implements.

Diagnostic Symptoms

Foliar Target Spotting

Older leaves nearest to the ground display irregular brown-to-black necrotic spots. Concentric rings develop within the spots creating a target-board pattern, often bordered by a chlorotic yellow halo.

Stem Collar Rot

Dark, sunken, elongated cankers form along seedling stems near the soil level, frequently causing girdling and plant collapse in younger transplants.

Stem-End Fruit Rot

Sunken, dark brown to leathery black lesions emerge at the calyx and stem attachment area, penetrating deep into the tomato fruit flesh.

Recommended Management & Remedies

Liquid Copper Octanoate Approved for Organic Farming

Dosage & Method: 10-15 ml per liter of water. Mist foliage until evenly wet at 7-day intervals starting prior to symptom outbreak.

Effectiveness: Forms a protective barrier inhibiting spore germination on the leaf surface.

Bio-Fungicide (Bacillus subtilis strain QST 713) Biological Formulation

Dosage & Method: Apply according to manufacturer specification as a preventive preventative foliar drench.

Effectiveness: Active bacterium colonizes leaf surfaces, displacing pathogenic fungal mycelium.

Chemical Safety: Always consult certified agricultural agronomists, respect pre-harvest intervals, and avoid spraying during flowering to protect pollinators.
Chlorothalonil (Bravo / Daconil) Broad Spectrum Contact

Dosage: 1.5 – 2.0 kg/ha in 500 liters of water. Spray every 7 to 10 days during humid periods.

Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): 1 day on field tomatoes.

Azoxystrobin (Heritage / Quadris) Systemic Strobilurin

Dosage: 250 - 300 ml/ha. Limit to 2 consecutive applications before alternating fungicide classes.

Resistance Management: Must alternate with multisite protectants (e.g. Mancozeb) to prevent resistance.

Agronomic Prevention
  • Strict Crop Rotation: Rotate out of Solanaceae (tomato, potato, eggplant, pepper) for 3–4 seasons.
  • Organic Mulching: Apply clean straw or plastic mulch to create a physical barrier preventing rain-splash from soil.
  • Drip Irrigation: Water only the root zone. Avoid overhead sprinklers that wet canopy foliage.
  • Lower Canopy Pruning: Prune suckers and foliage below the first fruit cluster to maintain canopy airflow.
  • Field Sanitation: Remove and destroy (do not compost) heavily blighted vines at the end of each harvest.
