Early Blight

Alternaria solani

High Severity

Early blight is a common fungal disease of tomato plants caused by Alternaria solani. It primarily attacks older foliage first, producing dark circular brown spots with characteristic concentric target-board rings. Unchecked infections cause severe defoliation, sunburn on fruit, and reduced yield.

Primary Observable Symptoms

  • • Brown to black spots on lower leaves with concentric yellow halos.
  • • Progressive yellowing of foliage surrounding infected lesions.
  • • Dark, sunken collar rot lesions on stems near the soil surface.
Curated Treatment Recommendations Phase 6 Integrated Shell
Copper Octanoate / Copper Fungicide Eco-Approved

Application: Apply dilute liquid spray (10-15 ml per liter of water) thoroughly wetting upper and lower leaf surfaces. Repeat every 7-10 days after rainy periods.

Notes: Suitable for organic tomato production. Apply early morning or dusk to prevent foliage burn.

Bacillus subtilis Bio-Fungicide Biological

Application: Spray foliar bio-fungicide according to label instructions at the earliest onset of leaf spotting.

Notes: Competitively colonizes foliage surfaces and inhibits spore germination naturally.

Follow safety intervals (PHI), wear personal protective equipment, and adhere to local agricultural chemical guidelines.
Chlorothalonil (Broad-Spectrum Protectant) Synthetic

Application: Apply at 1.5 - 2.0 kg/ha in 500 liters of water when conditions favor disease spread.

Notes: Preventive contact fungicide. Pre-harvest interval (PHI) is typically 0-1 days on tomatoes.

Mancozeb 75% WP Synthetic

Application: Foliar spray at 2.5 g/liter of water every 7–10 days.

Notes: Alternate with systemic fungicides to prevent pathogen resistance development.

Cultural Prevention Tips

  • • Drip irrigate at the base; keep tomato foliage dry whenever possible.
  • • Mulch around the base of plants to avoid soil splashing onto lower foliage.
  • • Prune lower leaves (bottom 12 inches) once plant is established to promote airflow.
  • • Rotate crops with non-solanaceous species on a minimum 3-year schedule.
